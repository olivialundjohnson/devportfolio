## The challenge

The U.S. electricity system is facing a new wave of demand growth, driven by
data centers and artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing, and
widespread electrification. Meeting this demand requires new infrastructure,
but it also raises difficult questions about who pays for grid expansion, how
utilities manage uncertainty, and how governments balance reliability,
affordability, and energy development.

## The research

For my capstone research at the Yale School of the Environment, I assessed
emerging state and federal policy responses to electricity load growth. I
reviewed legislative, regulatory, and planning initiatives across the country,
organizing them into a framework spanning cost allocation, grid flexibility,
interconnection reform, state energy planning, and permitting.

The assessment examined how different jurisdictions are approaching a shared
challenge, from requiring large electricity customers to shoulder more of the
costs associated with their demand to streamlining infrastructure development
and incorporating large loads into long-term grid planning.

## Why it matters

The policy decisions being made today will shape how the U.S. power system
responds to growing electricity demand. Understanding these emerging approaches
is essential to evaluating how new infrastructure gets built, how its costs are
distributed, and how governments navigate competing priorities around
reliability, affordability, and energy development.
